# World of Ender

*[Version française](README.fr.md)* · A **Minecraft 1.21.1 / NeoForge** mod · version **0.33.4**

You forge an obsidian door, you wake it at the cost of a ritual, and it opens
onto a world that was not there before. Behind it, an entire plot of land is
yours — and you carry it everywhere: the door dematerialises into your pocket and
sets itself down wherever you like.

## In short

* **A base you can carry.** A two-block door, placed with a single click, that
  holds an 8192-block plot in a dimension of its own.
* **A world to dig.** The Ender is solid from end to end — not one cave, not one
  tunnel. You move through it by carving your own gallery.
* **Storage at a distance.** Wherever you are, drop a stack into the chests back
  home with one right-click.
* **Sharing, by choice.** The Allies' Passage links two bases if both players
  want it — and you decide, friend by friend, what they may do in yours.
* **Translucent materials.** The Ender Block and its whole brick family: you
  build walls you can see through.

## The lore

The player has the Overworld, and beneath the Overworld, the Nether.
The Enderman has the End, and beneath the End, **the Ender**.

Endermen do not pick up blocks at random. Every block they lift is an offering:
matter taken out of the world, carried off to be given up — and what is given up
is sent to the Ender, where destroyed things come to rest. The paradise of cubes.

Your door does not open onto a new world. It opens onto theirs.

## Getting started

### 1. The Ender Crystal

**Ender Ore** generates **in End stone**, between **Y 10 and Y 70**, in small
clusters. It grows nowhere else, so the mod opens with a trip to the End.

An **iron pickaxe** is enough. The ore yields **Ender Crystals** — Fortune
increases the yield, Silk Touch collects the block intact. Everything else in the
mod is built from those crystals.

### 2. Forge the door

**7 crying obsidian + 1 crystal + 1 nether star** make an **Inactive Ender
Door**. At this stage it does nothing: it is a two-block casing you place and
take back with a pickaxe. It is asleep.

### 3. The Mace ritual

To wake it, you have to strike it hard enough to crack space itself.

Place the door, take a vanilla **Mace**, climb **at least 20 blocks** above it,
jump, and **hit it mid-fall** (left-click). It is the Mace's smash attack, aimed
at a door.

The impact absorbs your fall damage, lightning strikes, the door wakes. The Mace
is not consumed, only slightly worn.

> **One door per player.** The ritual refuses to wake a second one.

### 4. Forge the key

**1 crystal + 1 ender pearl + 1 gold ingot**, stacked in a column, make the
**Ender Key**. **Right-click in the air** to bind it to your door: it will obey
no one else.

### 5. The pickaxe

**3 crystals + 2 sticks** make the **Ender Pickaxe**. It is the only tool that
harvests Ender Blocks, and it breaks them almost instantly.

Failing that, the **"Spacetime Breach"** enchantment lets any pickaxe break them
with drops, at a decent speed. It is a **treasure** enchantment, like Mending:
you find it in loot or trade for it with a librarian, never at an enchanting
table.

## The door, day to day

| You do… | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Right-click the door, **bare-handed** | It opens, or closes. |
| Right-click the door, **holding the key** | The same — the key does not make you put it away. |
| **Key** + **sneak** + right-click **on the ground** | The door materialises where you aimed, and vanishes from wherever it stood before. |
| **Key** + **sneak** + right-click the door | It dematerialises: your base goes back into your pocket. |
| **Key** + right-click **in the air** | Binds the key to your door. |
| Right-click the **inner door** while the door is stowed | It rematerialises outside, open: that is your way out. |

What only the key can do is **make the door appear and disappear**, and bind
itself to it. Opening and closing is done by hand.

**All of this obeys the owner alone.** Anyone else clicking your door, with or
without a key, reads "locked".

**One gesture at a time.** While the fade lasts, the door turns the next order
down and answers "The door has not finished its passage." It obeys again the
moment it has finished reappearing.

### What you see during the fade

A **wave of light**, white with violet edges, climbs all four faces of the casing
from threshold to lintel — and travels back down on the way out. **Portal grains
converge** from a ring over a metre wide and tighten as the door takes hold; when
it leaves, everything flies back outwards. The door **shivers** for as long as it
is not quite there, then steadies: it does not snap to full opacity, it settles.
It **lands** on a ring of sparks, a deep impact and an amethyst resonance.

### The three safeguards

**Losing your key does not cost you your base.** Forge another and right-click in
the air: it binds to your door, wherever it is. Without that, a dematerialised
door would leave nothing to click on, and the ritual refuses to wake a second
one.

**The key obeys only you.** Dropped, lost or stolen, it is inert in anyone else's
hands: it materialises nothing, opens nothing, and will not bind itself to your
door.

**The recall cannot fail.** If someone built on the exact spot where your door
was sleeping while you were inside, the recall tries, in order: the remembered
spot, then a free space nearby (8 blocks around, 4 above or below), then your
respawn point. It gives you the coordinates if it had to move it.

> **Placing** the door from outside is allowed to refuse, on the other hand:
> there you chose the spot yourself, and you are not locked anywhere.

## The world of the Ender

A **wholly underground** world, carved out of a mass of Ender Blocks: grey,
translucent like a honey block, faintly luminous.

Through that matter you **catch sight of the relic blocks** that came here to
die — stone, logs, ores up to diamond, bookshelves, sponges. They are gathered in
**clouds** of a single material, so diffuse that no two blocks touch.

Rare **veins of pearlescent froglight** run through it in long trails of violet
light. They are the only landmarks — and the only light: everywhere else the
darkness is total. A pale ash falls without end.

**The mass is solid.** Not one cave, not one void. You move through it only with
the Ender Pickaxe, carving your own gallery.

### Your plot

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Size | **8192 blocks** across |
| World height | −64 to 320, closed top and bottom by bedrock |
| Arrival room | Y 64 |
| Beds | ✔ you sleep in them and set your respawn point |
| Respawn anchors | ✘ they explode, as in the Overworld |
| Nether portals | ✘ they will not light |

Plots are separated by a **grid of bedrock walls**, running floor to ceiling of
the world. You can dig in every direction without ever running into someone
else's base, or them into yours. The Allies' Passage is the door you deliberately
open in that wall.

The owner's name is shown on a small plate on the front of the door.

### Teleporting is refused

The mod rests on two gestures: **materialising your door** to get home, and
**opening an Allies' Passage** to visit someone else. A waypoint mod — Waystones
and its like — makes both of them pointless at a stroke: you land wherever you
want, the Ender included, and nothing needs opening or materialising any more.

Refused, therefore, whatever the source:

* any teleport that **enters** the Ender world;
* any teleport that **leaves** it;
* any jump that **crosses a plot wall**.

Still allowed: the mod's own trips — door, Allies' Passage, Entity Teleporter —
and **jumps inside your own plot**. An ender pearl, a chorus fruit or a waypoint
placed at home excuse you from nothing, since you had to get in first.

And above all: **any crossing that starts on the threshold of a door or a
passage**, whatever code carries it out. That is essential with Immersive
Portals, which turns the door into a real portal and moves the player itself,
never asking the mod. The rule is the one stated from the start — you only get in
through a door — and it is checked by looking at blocks, knowing nothing about
the mod on the other side.

The refusal rests on **two independent checks**, and each catches one half of
the problem.

The first one *prevents*. NeoForge announces every `changeDimension` before it
happens, and that one is cancelled. That is the path a **world change** takes:
Waystones calls `ServerPlayer#teleportTo`, which for a destination in another
world falls through to that method. The player simply does not move, with no
visible jolt.

The second one *undoes*. For a destination **in the same world**, that same
`teleportTo` writes straight to the client without announcing anything — no event
exists, so there is nothing to cancel. Every tick, each player's position is
therefore compared with the previous tick's, and crossing a plot wall sends them
back where they came from. The cost: one tick of latency, and a mod that has
already charged its price does not refund it.

Neither check would be enough on its own.

Two settings, in your save's `serverconfig/enderportals-server.toml`:

| Setting | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `blockTeleports` | `true` | The refusal described above |
| `allowOperatorTeleports` | `false` | Operators are not affected |

> **Why the operator bypass defaults to false.** On most servers the person
> playing is also the person running it: at `true`, the refusal applies to nobody
> and the mod looks like it does nothing. Set it to `true` only when you need to
> move a player by command — while it is `false`, a `/tp` into the Ender is
> refused to operators like anyone else.
>
> The option was called `operatorBypass` in 0.31.0 and defaulted to `true`. The
> name changed so that existing saves benefit: a config file already on disk
> keeps its values, and only an unknown key gets replaced by the new one with its
> default. Nothing for you to do.

Every refusal leaves a line in the server log, naming which of the two checks
acted. If the guard looks inactive, that is the first place to look.

## Building

**4 crystals** make an **Ender Block**; **4 Ender Blocks** make **4 Ender
Bricks**. The family comes in **stairs, slabs, a wall** and a **chiselled**
variant engraved with the eye of the End portal frame — all of it also cut on the
stonecutter.

The bricks are **translucent like the block they come from**: a wall lets you
make out what is behind it, and a thick wall does not darken layer by layer. In
hand and in the inventory they are **solid**, on the other hand: seeing the
landscape through the cube you are holding looked like nothing at all.

## Storage at a distance

Two items turn your base into a warehouse you can reach from anywhere.

**The Item Transmitter** (7 iron blocks, 1 redstone block, 1 ender chest) is
placed **inside the Ender**, against your storage. It federates the whole
adjacent network, block by block.

**The Ender Bag** (a bundle + an ender chest) stays in your inventory. The
gesture works **both ways**:

* pick a stack onto the cursor, then **right-click the bag**;
* or keep the bag on the cursor, and **right-click a stack**.

The stack goes into the network, wherever you are. **3 experience points per
stack** — the price of the trip, not of the weight: sixty-four blocks cost as
much as a single item. Network full or not enough experience: nothing leaves, the
stack stays on the cursor, a dry sound says so and the message gives the reason.

> **Mind the second direction.** You move a bag around your inventory more often
> than you file things away, and during that move a right-click on a stack sends
> it off.

### It plugs into your storage mod

The Transmitter does not replace your storage system, it **connects to it**: it
recognises any storage exposing an inventory through the NeoForge standard, so in
practice nearly every mod.

* **Sophisticated Storage** — chests, barrels, shulkers of every tier, and the
  Storage Controller.
* **Sophisticated Backpacks** — backpacks placed on the ground.
* **Tom's Simple Storage** — put the Transmitter against an Inventory Connector:
  the Bag fills the whole network, and the Storage Terminal shows your items,
  counted once.
* Vanilla chests and barrels, drawers, and the rest.

Because the deposit goes through standard insertion, it **respects each storage's
filters and upgrades**: a filtered Sophisticated barrel sorts what you pour in.

## The Entity Teleporter

The key only takes you. To bring an animal into the Ender you need two pieces:

* **The Entity Lander** (6 crying obsidian, 2 crystals, 1 slime block) is placed
  **inside the Ender**, where the animal should arrive.
* **The Entity Teleporter** (5 crying obsidian, 1 ender pearl) is a hull you
  place and board **like a boat**.

**Three steps.** Holding the teleporter, **right-click a Lander**: the hull
remembers that spot. **Set it down** where you like and **lure the animal
aboard**. **Right-click the hull**: it leaves.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Green lamp** | destination *and* passenger: the hull can go |
| **Red lamp** | one of the two is missing |
| **Price** | 20 experience points **per creature**, taken on arrival |
| **Right-click when empty** | puts the hull back in your hand, with its link |
| **Sneak + right-click** | picks it up **and lets out** whatever it carries |

The lamp speaks of the machine, not of the ground: knowing whether the Lander is
still there would mean keeping its chunk loaded at all times. That check happens
at departure, and you are told if it is gone.

### Charging, and leaving

The trip does not start on the click: the chunks around the Lander are
**requested, then waited for**, which takes anywhere from nothing to a few
seconds. The machine makes a show of that wait.

A **ring of light** travels up the hull on a loop — the same light as the door's
wave. The hull **draws in**: grains converge, and the four crystals each spit a
spark in turn. A **note climbs**, higher and higher, until the ground is ready.

On departure the hull leaves on a **column of sparks** and a violet burst
collapsing into the spot it just left — which is what you see, since you stay on
this side. If the trip is refused after charging began, the ring goes out on a
**dry click**. Clicking again while it charges restarts nothing.

Once landed, hull and passenger are **a single record** in the save: the chunk
can unload behind them without anything being lost, exactly like a pig in a boat
on the far side of the world.

## The Allies' Passage

The **Allies' Passage** (4 quartz blocks, 4 crystals, 1 ender chest) is an arch
placed **inside your plot**. The **Friendship Control** (6 quartz, 2 redstone,
1 crystal) is placed **against it**: that is what commands it.

**Place as many passages as you have friends to link.** Each arch carries the
name of the ally it leads to, and each Control commands the arch it touches. A
closed arch is an arch bound to nobody, and it carries no name.

### 1. Becoming friends

Every awakened door receives an **eight-digit code**, every digit between 1 and 9
— which is why the keypad has no zero. You read it on your key's tooltip and
under the Control's keypad.

Read yours out, type in theirs, **and have them do the same**: friendship is the
conjunction of two declarations. While only one has typed, the name shows with an
**hourglass**.

### 2. Opening the passage

**Click a friend's name** in the list: a request goes out, valid for **two
minutes**. Their name blinks orange on their side; they click in turn, and the
two arches open onto each other. The border turns **green**.

To close, click the green-bordered name — from any of your Controls. **Shift +
click** removes the friend from the list.

### 3. The terminal

The bottom of the panel carries the device's log: requests, openings, closings,
refusals. A **lamp** in its header says whether this Control commands a passage
at all, and a **status line** at the bottom gives the reason when it does not —
panel placed away from any arch, arch free, arch broken at the other end.

Messages received while you were away are waiting the next time you open it.

## Degrees of trust

Opening a passage is not giving everything away. To the right of each name, as
soon as the link is **green**, a badge says what that ally may do **at your
place**. Click it: it steps to the next degree, and wraps back to the first.

| Degree | Walk in | Use your blocks | Break and build |
| --- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Visitor** | ✔ | | |
| **Guest** | ✔ | ✔ | |
| **Partner** | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |

"Use" covers **anything that opens or triggers**: chests, furnaces, crafting
tables, levers. A visitor does not touch those — but they do **get around**:
doors, trapdoors, fence gates, buttons and pressure plates stay open to them.

Three things to know:

* **The degree runs one way.** Opening my chests to you does not oblige me to
  open yours. Each of you sets their own plot, on their own panel.
* **It survives the passage closing.** Asking for it again on every reopening
  would have turned a setting into a chore — and pushed everyone to leave partner
  as the default.
* **Everyone starts as a visitor**, and **there are no exceptions**, not even for
  operators. An admin still has creative mode and commands, which do not go
  through this.

The ally is told on their terminal on every change. Forgetting an ally resets the
degree both ways.

> **What is guarded, and what is not.** The three direct routes are held:
> breaking, placing, using. A tamed animal killed, an item frame emptied or TNT
> lit from outside the plot are all still possible. That is a stated scope, not a
> watertightness claimed.
>
> **If you close the passage while a friend is inside**, they leave through your
> inner door — if it is open. Closed, they cannot open it, and your plot is walled
> in bedrock. Close up once they are home.

The list of what a visitor may use is the `enderportals:visitor_usable` tag, built
from vanilla tags: modded doors that join `#minecraft:doors` come along for free,
and a pack that wants levers in there has one line to write.

### With Immersive Portals

If it is installed, both the door and the passage become **see-through** portals:
you glimpse the other side before crossing the threshold. Without it, the mod
falls back on its own teleport-on-contact.

### Advancements

A full tree, **"The paradise of cubes"**:

*A stone not from here* → *The threshold* → *Striking the sky* → *The paradise of
cubes*, then five branches — *Carving your gallery* (a thousand Ender Blocks),
*The bottom of the world*, *The warehouse* → *Filing it all away*, *Building in
the translucent*, and *Friend in passing*.

### Tooltips

Every machine here has enough to fill five or six lines. So the instructions only
unfold while you **hold SHIFT**, the way storage mods do it.

What stays visible without pressing anything is **the state of that particular
item**: the door this key is bound to and its friend code, the Lander this
teleporter is aiming at. That is what tells two items apart in a chest.

**Recipe viewers see everything.** JEI, EMI and REI read tooltips to index them,
and their search only finds what they were able to read: folded behind a key
nobody is holding at indexing time, the instructions were invisible to them. They
now announce that they want the full version, and they get it — searching for
"lander" brings up the teleporter aiming at one. This is what sets the floor at
**NeoForge 21.1.238**.

## Server and compatibility

The mod is **server-authoritative**: all the logic — ritual, key, passages,
storage, permissions — runs on the server, and the client only draws. It works as
is on a dedicated server.

It is also built to share a pack: **no mixins**, purely additive vanilla tags,
event listeners scoped to its own blocks and its own dimension, and a
self-contained dimension generator. The key and the ritual respect **spawn
protection**, **adventure mode** and **land-claim mods**.

**Immersive Portals** is optional: present, it gives see-through portals; absent,
or if its API changes, the mod falls back on its classic teleport on its own and
says so in the logs.

## Shaders

The Ender declares itself to shaders **as the Nether**: that is what gives it its
underground look, with no horizon and no sky, with its fog and its ash storm.
**There is nothing to configure.** Tested with Complementary Reimagined and
Photon.

Your gaze carries through the translucent mass, so it carries very far. A fade to
black closes the view before the chunk limit — you will never see the edge of the
loaded world. Now and then that distance flares for a fraction of a second:
silent storm light.

**One Photon setting is worth knowing**: *Light Sources →
**NETHER_USE_BIOME_COLOR***. Enabled — its default — Photon takes the colour the
world gives it, which is ours. Disabled, it falls back on its Nether orange and
the Ender turns to fire. Leave it as it is.

**Want a depth blur?** Complementary provides one, off by default: *Camera
Settings → World Blur → World Blur → **Distance Blur***, then the strength on the
*"Dis. Blur — The Nether"* slider.

### Forcing another render pass

Nothing obliges you to keep the Nether's. One line in the shaderpack's
`dimension.properties` (in its `shaders/` folder, to be created if absent)
redirects the world wherever you want — Photon, for instance, has volumetric fog
dedicated to the End:

```properties
dimension.world1=minecraft:the_end enderportals:ender_world
dimension.world0=*
```

That explicit mapping wins over everything else: it is the only place from which
one can change one's mind, since a mod cannot write into a shaderpack.

### A pause on the first crossing

The very first time the door opens in a session, the screen freezes for two to
four seconds: that is **Iris compiling its render pass** for a dimension that had
never appeared, on the render thread, and no mod has a hand in it. Immersive
Portals makes it visible by creating the destination world while you are still
outside — the same compilation happens for a Nether portal, but there it hides
behind the travel loading screen.

It is paid **once per session**, and not at all without shaders.

## Installing and building

Put the jar in `mods/`, with **NeoForge 21.1.238** or newer, for **Minecraft 1.21.1**. Every
build is published to the [`dev-latest`](../../releases/tag/dev-latest)
pre-release.

To build — **Java 21** required:

```bash
./gradlew build
# → build/libs/enderportals-0.33.4.jar
```

The build is handled by **ModDevGradle**; NeoForge and the official mappings are
downloaded automatically, versions pinned in `gradle.properties`.

> The mod is called *World of Ender*, but its internal id is still
> `enderportals` — it is written into every block, item, recipe and existing
> save. Changing it would break every world already created.
